Where your journal goes

The journal lives on the phone. It leaves the phone only encrypted, or as a page you chose to share. Cairn's servers hold accounts and sync bookkeeping, never journal text.

What crosses each boundary

DataPathCan Cairn read it?
Pages, pocket items, photosPhone → R2 journal storage, or your own iCloud / Google DriveNo. Encrypted on the phone; the key never leaves your devices.
Location pointsSaved on the phone each time you open Cairn or capture something; synced like pagesNo. They are journal content, encrypted with everything else, and removed from shared pages.
Account and sync metadataPhone → cairn-api → PostgresYes: account id, device ids, sync cursors, blob ids and sizes. No journal text, titles or places.
A page you sharePhone → R2 shared pages → link viewerYes. You published it. Exact campsites are hidden by default.
Weather lookupPhone → Open-Meteo, directlyCairn never sees it. Open-Meteo gets a location rounded to about 1 km, the elevation and a date, with no account attached.
Elevation lookupPhone → terrain tiles, directlyCairn never sees it. The tile server learns only which ~10 km tile was downloaded; the exact elevation is read on the phone.
PurchasesApp Store / Google Play → RevenueCat → cairn-apiOnly which plan you have.
Sign-inApple / Google → cairn-apiOnly a verified identity token.
Printing (v2)Phone → cairn-api → LuluYes, for the notebook you order, only to print it.
ExportPhone → your filesNever touches Cairn.

Which repo owns what

RepoOwns
cairn-appEverything in the Phone box: the Flutter app, the local database, the sync engine and encryption, the Swift and Kotlin extensions, and the API client generated from a pinned spec.
cairn-apiThe API service, the Postgres schema and migrations, signing R2 upload URLs, the RevenueCat webhook, share and print endpoints.
cairn-infraThe existence of the Cairn Cloud boxes: Fly.io apps, the Neon project, R2 buckets, DNS and secrets, for staging and prod.
cairnThe product hub: the sync and encryption format spec (what is inside the blobs), decision records, and this diagram.

Decisions this diagram records

  • The phone is the source of truth. Every storage option is a copy of the local journal, so the app works the same with no account and no signal.
  • Blobs bypass the API. The phone uploads straight to R2 with short-lived signed URLs, so the API never handles journal bytes, encrypted or not.
  • One writer per page. Even on shared trips each person owns their own pages, so sync resolves conflicts with "last write wins" per record and needs no merge logic.
  • Location points are journal content. Cairn saves one location fix each time you open the app or capture something, never continuously. You choose Always (so the Action button and widget work while the phone is locked), While using, or Off. Points are encrypted like pages, shown in the margin as they are saved, and can be deleted or paused. With location off, the journal still works and those points are simply missing.
  • Weather and elevation are looked up by the phone. Open-Meteo gets a rounded location plus the exact elevation, and terrain tiles are read on the phone, so a hiker's exact position never reaches Cairn's servers or anyone else's.
  • Own-cloud storage skips Cairn. With iCloud or Google Drive, encrypted files go from the phone to the user's account and Cairn's servers are not in the path.
